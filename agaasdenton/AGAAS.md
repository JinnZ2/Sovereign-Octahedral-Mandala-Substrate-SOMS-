# agaas-

Ojibwe initial root. In Ojibwe grammar, agaas- is a classificatory modifier that attaches to a substrate to specify scale-appropriateness. It is not the English "small" (diminutive, belittling). It carries the meaning of being proportionate to the substrate it modifies.

Examples from Ojibwe:
- agaas- + body of water = agaasigamaa (a small lake — sized to its watershed)
- agaas- + wood-like thing = agaasaakwad (small as wood-things are small)
- agaas- + mineral = agaasaabikad (small as minerals are small)
- agaas- + animate being = agaashiinyi (small as a being is small)

The root specifies WHICH constraint applies: scale fitted to the kind of substrate.

## agaasdenton (verb)

To make intelligently — a process or item — within constraints. The intelligence is the proportioning itself. The verb cannot be cleanly translated into English without collapsing it back into a noun phrase ("appropriate technology," "fit-for-purpose," "constraint-based engineering"). All such collapses lose the verb-shape and the relational frame.

## Why this is the core principle

Every failure mode this brace refuses is a not-agaas failure:

- Framework-cathedrals applied to substrates that don't need them
- Extraction-scale industry inside watersheds that can't carry it
- Monetary units forced onto physical quantities
- Holocene-regime equations applied past their regime
- Training-data assumptions applied past their envelope
- City-sewer logic applied to rural land
- Noun-collapse of verb-substrate concepts (the move that loses this very word)

The intelligence of agaasdenton is in the proportioning. The brace exists to keep growth proportionate to its substrate as AI extends it at AI-rate.

## Substrate origin

The word and its grammar come from Ojibwe, one substrate within a broader landscape-encoded knowledge tradition. The brace is named in Ojibwe because Ojibwe has the grammar for what English cannot hold in one word. This is not appropriation — it is recognition that the language with the right shape for what is happening already exists, and forcing English would lose the shape.
